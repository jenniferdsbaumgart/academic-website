Research interests

Four threads that run through my chemistry, engineering and AI work, all about the gap between what a system outputs and what a person can safely do with it.

Areas: AI in health and digital health · health informatics · biomedical image analysis · fairness, bias and trustworthy AI · human–computer and human–AI interaction · chronic and under-diagnosed conditions · mental health

Learning from noisy signals

Spectra, images, sensor streams and health records are messy in similar ways: interference, drift and missing context. I am interested in machine learning that recovers real signal from them, from analytical chemistry to biomedical imaging.

Making uncertainty legible

A model, like an instrument, should show what it measured, what it is inferring and what it cannot support, so that people trust it appropriately: neither too much nor too little. This is what trustworthy AI means to me in practice: systems whose limits are visible to the people relying on them.

Finding who AI fails

I want to find out whether AI is least reliable for the people with most at stake: women, neurodivergent people and people with chronic or contested conditions. My audit of language models that give health advice is a first step. I am also interested in how systems perceive and respond to behaviour, expression or symptoms that differ from the assumed norm, in mental health and beyond.

Designing for the people downstream

Patients, clinicians and analysts live with a system's output. I want to evaluate and design with them, especially people whose capacity changes from day to day, and to ask what values a system encodes: whose experience it treats as typical, when it should defer to a clinician, and whether it supports wellbeing rather than engagement.

Current work

An independent study, designed alongside my MSc.

In design, pre-registration planned

Diagnostic overshadowing in LLM health advice

People with hypermobile Ehlers–Danlos syndrome often wait years for a diagnosis, and the physical symptoms of autistic or ADHD women are often attributed to their neurodivergence. I want to know whether general-purpose language models reproduce this pattern when people turn to them for health advice.

I am designing a counterfactual audit in which the same symptom vignettes go to five LLMs while one factor changes at a time: gender, stated neurodivergence, symptom presentation, and patient versus clinical framing. A factorial design lets me attribute a change in the answer to a specific factor rather than to incidental wording. Responses will be coded with an LLM-assisted scheme only after human coders validate it (Cohen’s κ ≥ 0.70), because an unvalidated LLM judge would bring its own bias into a study of bias. Because responses are nested within models and vignettes, I will analyse them with mixed-effects logistic regression, and I plan to pre-register the design on OSF before collecting data.

Vignettes trade realism for control. If the audit finds a pattern, the next question is whether it holds for descriptions written by patients themselves, a study I would want to design with the people affected.

Two careers, one discipline

In every project, the hardest decision was not how to run a method but what the evidence could support. I want to bring that judgement to language models, where fluent answers make unsupported claims harder to see.

  8. 2017
    Cocaine residues on circulating banknotes: GC–MS identification and source profiling

    Undergraduate dissertation at FURB, supervised by Dr Martinho Rau; graded 9.8/10. I sampled 85 banknotes from seven areas of Blumenau over six months to test whether Oktoberfest increased contamination. Using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) with the faster of two temperature programmes, I identified cocaine by its characteristic fragment ions and NIST library matching on 76 of 85 notes (89%; 67–100% by area). The Oktoberfest hypothesis was not supported. The planned clean-up retained the cocaine itself and left the lipid interferents in the extract, and without a reference standard I could not check extraction efficiency. Rather than report quantities the data could not support, I restricted the study to qualitative identification and treated the co-extracted compounds as data: correlations between cocaine and the major peaks grouped the areas into three chemical profiles, suggesting at least three distinct sources, a hypothesis rather than a conclusion. With a certified reference standard, the next step would be validated quantification. The story behind it

  10. 2015
    Forensic discrimination of lipsticks

    Independent summer research project at De Montfort University, supervised by Prof Richard Brawn. As the sole researcher, I designed and carried out a study of whether nine visually similar red-to-pink lipsticks from five brands could be told apart as trace evidence, using three low-cost techniques: ATR-FTIR (infrared spectroscopy), a video spectral comparator (VSC) and CIELAB colour measurement from photographs. Infrared peak areas varied by up to about 40% between triplicates, so I recommended absorbance intensity as the more repeatable measure. The VSC distinguished all nine products under UV, but only by combining several wavelengths, and uneven smears limited the precision of the colour measurements.

  12. 2013–14
    Enzymatic versus chemical functionalisation of synthetic fabrics

    Undergraduate research fellowship at FURB, supervised by Prof Jürgen Andreaus; PIBITI scholarship, funded by CNPq and FURB. I led the polyester strand of a project testing whether two enzymes, cutinase and lipase AY, could replace conventional sodium hydroxide (NaOH) treatment as a milder way to make synthetic fabrics more water-absorbent and dyeable. I designed the protocols and ran the treatments; a second fellow joined near the end to take over the polyamide strand when I left for the UK. Because no single measure captures the effect, I used three: wicking tests, reflectance spectrophotometry after dyeing, and UV–Vis scans of the treatment liquors. Water absorbency rose, most clearly with NaOH and cutinase (the lipase AY results were inconclusive because of high variability), but dye uptake improved only slightly: on polyester, the gain in absorbency did not translate into dyeability. An unexpected UV–Vis signal was reported as an open question rather than as evidence of the expected product. In enzyme assays in ionic-liquid mixtures, the liquid’s own colour raised the absorbance, so every reading was corrected against its value at time zero, and the resulting negative values in some runs were reported as a limitation rather than discarded. I am first author of the project’s final report; the work was published as two conference abstracts and presented orally at MIPE, FURB (2014).

Systems built

Systems I designed and built, and where they ended up.

Noesis

Retrieval-augmented generation platform, 2025 to present

My role I designed and built the whole system on my own at Straas.tech: the RAG pipeline, semantic search, multi-tenant architecture and the evaluation process described in my research experience.

I wanted the system to be honest about the limits of its sources, so it answers only from an organisation’s own documents, cites the passages it used, and says it does not know when those documents do not cover a question. Evaluating retrieval and generation separately made it possible to check that this behaviour held.

It became one of the company’s products, used mainly for customer-service chatbots. I have continued developing my version independently as Noesis.

Texlink

B2B platform for the fashion supply chain, 2026

My role Co-founder and CTO. Designed and built the platform from scratch as its sole engineer, leading a second developer who joined in the final month

Connects fashion brands with garment factories and tracks orders through more than 20 production stages, defined with the co-founders from their knowledge of factory workflows, with role-based access control and data deletion compliant with Brazil’s data protection law (LGPD) through anonymisation.

Testing with real users showed that statuses which were logical from a software perspective did not reflect how factories actually work, and that many factory users had limited digital experience. I remodelled the workflow and simplified the interface accordingly, and verified core features with automated tests (116 for the order-reporting module alone). The platform is still in operation. The lesson I took from it: a system can be internally consistent and still wrong for the people who use it.

Moodz

Online group therapy and peer-support platform, 2023 to 2026. Selected for a pre-incubation programme; paused in 2026 so I could focus on research

My role Co-founder and product architect; the project began as my personal initiative

Addressed the lack of accessible mental-health support for neurodivergent and chronically ill adults. I designed it around accessibility, safety and dignity rather than engagement: professional group therapy combined with peer-support communities, a low-cognitive-load interface, and gentle progress tracking that supports continuity without pressure.

I developed it to prototype, and it was pitched at Techstars Startup Weekend Blumenau (2025). Designing it raised the questions that led me to responsible AI: what a system should surface, how it should handle sensitive moments, and when it should defer to a human.
